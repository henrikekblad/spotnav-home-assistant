SpotNav for Home Assistant 2.3.0 plans a target on the energy your car really takes per percent, lets solar charging use the charger's whole current and closes the load-balancing gaps found with a grid-charging home battery.

## New

- **A target counts on the energy your car really takes per percent.** A target's need was the battery size times the points to go, divided by a fixed efficiency of 0.9; a battery size that is off or a car that loses more in the cold made the plan buy too little time or too much. SpotNav now learns, per car, the energy from the wall one percent of its battery takes, from planned charges with a fresh reading of the car's level at both ends and at least 10 points between them. Once three such charges agree within 10 %, a target's need, what a plan short of the departure says the car reaches, the card's need and the level estimated between the car's readings use it, so the charge is not stopped early on an estimate. A fixed amount of kWh is never changed. There is no setting; *Download diagnostics* shows the learned figure and its samples.

## Fixes

- **Solar charging goes past 16 A.** The sun never asked a charger for more than 16 A, whatever the charger could do: a 32 A single-phase charger took at most 3.7 kW of sun and sent the rest to the grid, a 32 A three-phase one at most 11 kW. The sun now goes up to the charger's own maximum, the same top as the card's current slider (32 A when the charger states none), and never above the charging current set in Auto, as a planned charge. On a site that knows its main fuse the sun never asks for more than each of the car's phases can carry within it, also with load balancing off (a single-phase car on a site where the sun is spread over three phases), and a charger whose maximum changes, or that switches to one phase, is followed at once.
- **A home battery that takes what the car frees no longer gets the car stepped back above the fuse.** With *the battery gives way* on, a car lowered while the battery charged and filled the room it left was stepped straight back up, on a battery never seen to give way to the car, and the site read the fuse plus that step for one reading. The step back up now stays within the fuse, as every other raise does, until the battery has shown that it gives way.
- **A charge that starts as the home battery stops charging is no longer paused.** Right after a start the car is reckoned at what the site meter shows it took, since the charger's own reading lags. When the battery stopped charging in the same seconds, the site rose by much less than the car drew, the rest looked like house load, and the car was paused five seconds after its start. What the battery stopped drawing since the start now counts as the car's too.
- **A pause that comes while a start is still on its way holds.** When load balancing found no room for the car's minimum before the charger had answered a start, nothing was sent, the start landed and the car charged against the pause; the pause was then not known as load balancing's, so the charge was not resumed by itself and SpotNav said it had not started as planned. The pause now stops the start and keeps the charge waiting for room, and it is resumed as any balancing pause is.
- **A charge paused for load balancing is resumed after one minute, not three.** An OCPP charger sends one last meter value a few seconds after the stop, still showing the car's current, and nothing after it. That value held the resume back until it was two minutes old. It now counts as the charge before the pause.
- **A step up that the grid has room for is no longer taken back.** With *the battery gives way* on, a step up the battery did not make room for was taken back even when the site stayed within its limit. It is now taken back only when it took the site over the limit.

## Going back

- **Going back to 2.2 works out a target's need with the fixed efficiency again.** 2.2 does not read the learned energy per percent; it is kept and used again when you update.
