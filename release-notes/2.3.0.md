SpotNav for Home Assistant 2.3.0 shows a plan before you save it, plans a target on the energy your car really takes per percent, prices a grid fee that is a percentage of the spot price or higher in high-load hours, lets solar charging use the charger's whole current and closes the load-balancing gaps found with a grid-charging home battery.

## New

- **The Plan dialog shows what the plan would be before you save.** While you change the amount, the target, the departure, the weekdays or the current, a line under the fields says what a Save would plan, for example *00:00–01:48 · 20 kWh · 56.65 kr · ready 01:48*, or why no plan can be made (it does not make the departure, prices are awaited, the area is missing), in the same words as the card's status. Under it a thin strip shows the hours to the departure, each quarter-hour coloured by price as the chart colours it and the planned ones marked. It follows your edits a moment after you stop; nothing is saved, sent to the charger or notified until you press *Save*, and *Cancel* leaves everything as it was. It is the very plan a Save makes, with the learned power and an amount already delivered counted as they would be. It is shown to whoever may change the plan; a reader without *Save* does not see it.
- **A target counts on the energy your car really takes per percent.** A target's need was the battery size times the points to go, divided by a fixed efficiency of 0.9; a battery size that is off or a car that loses more in the cold made the plan buy too little time or too much. SpotNav now learns, per car, the energy from the wall one percent of its battery takes, from planned charges with a fresh reading of the car's level at both ends and at least 10 points between them. Once three such charges agree within 10 %, a target's need, what a plan short of the departure says the car reaches, the card's need and the level estimated between the car's readings use it, so the charge is not stopped early on an estimate. A fixed amount of kWh is never changed. There is no setting; *Download diagnostics* shows the learned figure and its samples.

- **A grid fee that is a percentage of the spot price.** Some network operators charge part of the grid fee as a percentage of the spot price (for example 20 öre/kWh plus 5 % of spot). The electricity price has a new addition, *Grid fee, % of spot*, off until you set it. It is added before VAT, so with 36 öre of tax, a fixed grid fee of 16 öre without VAT and 25 % VAT, a spot price of 80 öre costs (1.05 × 80 + 36 + 16) × 1.25 = 170 öre. It follows the spot price's sign: at a spot price of -10 öre the percentage is -0.5 öre, and the price is 51.9 öre. The order of the hours does not change, so plans stay where they were; the plan's cost, the price now and the charge history follow.
- **A grid fee that is higher in high-load hours.** For a time-of-use grid tariff, *Grid fee in high-load hours* is the grid fee inside a high-load window, in place of the ordinary one there. You set the window first, on the area's own clock: every day, weekdays or Monday to Saturday; a run of months, both included, that may cross the new year (November to March, October to March, or all year); and whole hours, which may cross midnight (22–06). A night's hours belong to the day the window starts on, so weekdays 22–06 holds Friday night until Saturday 06:00 but not Sunday night. The high-load fee is refused until there is a window. Public holidays count as the weekdays they fall on. A plan then avoids the window where that is cheaper, the electricity price's *Cheap now* and *Expensive now* hours follow, and on the days the clocks change the window keeps to the clock on the wall.
- **In the card's Settings.** The price section shows *Grid fee, % of spot* and *Grid fee in high-load hours* under the grid fee, and the high-load hours on one line ("Weekdays 07–19, Nov–Mar", "Mon–Sat 07–22, Nov–Mar", "Weekdays 22–06, all year") while that fee is on. Turning the high-load fee on with no hours set asks for the hours first, filled in with weekdays 07–19, November to March for you to check against your network operator's tariff, then the fee; nothing is saved until both are confirmed.
- **Set on the electricity price.** Both are part of the electricity price, so every charger that prices with it follows at once. In Home Assistant the two figures are numbers on the electricity price device; their on/off choices and the window's days, months and hours are there too, hidden by default. The app shows and sets them in a later release. Neither addition has a suggestion, and both are locked where the area's published price already includes the grid fee (Great Britain's Agile, Spain's PVPC). Moving the electricity price to another area switches the high-load fee off, as its figure is in the old area's unit; the figure is kept, so switching it on again is one step.
- **The sun may fill past the target, up to the car's own limit.** Under Solar and Hybrid the target is what must be in the car by the departure, bought from the grid if needed. Once it is reached, surplus now keeps charging the car up to the charge limit the car states, never from the grid: Hybrid's grid part still stops at the target, and the rest runs only on the sun's usual rules (start and stop delays, phase switching, load balancing, charger priority). The status says *Target reached – the sun tops up to the car's limit of 90 %*, and the energy counts as solar in the charge history. A car whose limit is not known, or a plan for an amount of energy, stops at the target as before; Cheapest is unchanged. There is no setting.
- **A charger's own current sensors are offered for the site wiring.** Many integrations ship the sensors that measure a charger's current disabled (Peblar's *Current phase 1–3*), so nothing was filled in and solar ran blind at the minimum current. The card's site editor now offers them under *Found in Home Assistant* for a charger whose wiring reads none; **Use** enables the ones the integration disabled and sets them in the wiring. A sensor you disabled yourself is never enabled. The status line and the charger's dialog name the sensors in place of the fixed "e.g. Easee Current".

## Fixes

- **Solar charging goes past 16 A.** The sun never asked a charger for more than 16 A, whatever the charger could do: a 32 A single-phase charger took at most 3.7 kW of sun and sent the rest to the grid, a 32 A three-phase one at most 11 kW. The sun now goes up to the charger's own maximum, the same top as the card's current slider (32 A when the charger states none), and never above the charging current set in Auto, as a planned charge. On a site that knows its main fuse the sun never asks for more than each of the car's phases can carry within it, also with load balancing off (a single-phase car on a site where the sun is spread over three phases), and a charger whose maximum changes, or that switches to one phase, is followed at once.
- **A home battery that takes what the car frees no longer gets the car stepped back above the fuse.** With *the battery gives way* on, a car lowered while the battery charged and filled the room it left was stepped straight back up, on a battery never seen to give way to the car, and the site read the fuse plus that step for one reading. The step back up now stays within the fuse, as every other raise does, until the battery has shown that it gives way.
- **A charge that starts as the home battery stops charging is no longer paused.** Right after a start the car is reckoned at what the site meter shows it took, since the charger's own reading lags. When the battery stopped charging in the same seconds, the site rose by much less than the car drew, the rest looked like house load, and the car was paused five seconds after its start. What the battery stopped drawing since the start now counts as the car's too.
- **A pause that comes while a start is still on its way holds.** When load balancing found no room for the car's minimum before the charger had answered a start, nothing was sent, the start landed and the car charged against the pause; the pause was then not known as load balancing's, so the charge was not resumed by itself and SpotNav said it had not started as planned. The pause now stops the start and keeps the charge waiting for room, and it is resumed as any balancing pause is.
- **A charge paused for load balancing is resumed after one minute, not three.** An OCPP charger sends one last meter value a few seconds after the stop, still showing the car's current, and nothing after it. That value held the resume back until it was two minutes old. It now counts as the charge before the pause.
- **A step up that the grid has room for is no longer taken back.** With *the battery gives way* on, a step up the battery did not make room for was taken back even when the site stayed within its limit. It is now taken back only when it took the site over the limit.
- **Removing a charger from SpotNav hands it back.** A charger SpotNav had stopped stayed stopped after its entry was deleted, and one held at a lower current stayed there. Deleting the entry now turns the charger back on and, where SpotNav set its current, sets it to the top of its range, once, also for a charger that keeps the current in flash. A restart, a reload or a disabled entry changes nothing.
- **The card heals itself after an update.** A browser or the Companion app could keep the old card after SpotNav was updated; it then said *The integration answered with something this card cannot read*, in English. When the card cannot read an answer, or cannot fetch its language file, it now asks which card SpotNav serves. If a newer one is served it says *SpotNav has been updated – reloading…* and reloads the page once to load it; if the old card is still there after that reload, where the browser cannot remember the reload, or while a dialog holds unsaved input, it shows a *Reload* button instead, so it can never reload in a loop or lose what you typed. If the card is the one served, the answer is taken as passing (Home Assistant restarting) and read again after a few seconds before the card says it cannot read it. There is no setting.

## Not covered yet

- **One high-load window and one high-load fee.** A tariff whose peak fee differs by season is set with the season that matters (for example October to March), and the other months pay the ordinary grid fee. A window with half hours, separate day and night fees beyond one window, and a percentage of the month's average spot price (SpotNav takes each interval's own) are not covered. A demand charge (a fee per kW of the month's peak) comes later.

## For app and card developers

- The dashboard has an additive `grid_tariff` block beside `price_source`, sent over the webhook only to an app that asks for it (`"reads": ["grid_tariff"]`), and one write: `spotnav/update_grid_tariff` over the WebSocket and the webhook action `update_grid_tariff`. The settings record is unchanged: its `overrides` rows keep their four keys, and a full settings write from a client that does not know the grid tariff leaves it as it is. `api_version` stays 1. See [Apps and API](../docs/api.md).
- A new status line, `sun_past_target` (`percent`, the car's limit), says the sun fills past a reached target. The webhook sends it only to an app that asks for it (`"reads": ["sun_past_target"]`); an older app gets `hybrid_satisfied` in its place under Hybrid, and the solar headline alone under Solar.
- The webhook answers three more actions, so the app can do what the card does: `plan_preview` (the plan a Save would make, with the command's checks and limits), `dismiss_setup_item` (a "Get started" row's "Not needed") and `fix_own_mode` (set the charger's own setting back, only to the fix of a conflict the charger has now). The dashboard's `charger.capabilities` says so with `plan_preview`, `dismiss_setup_item` and `fix_own_mode`. The status lines `charger_own_mode` and `charger_disabled` carry, additively, Home Assistant's name for the entity and the display names of its state and of the value that ends it (`entity_name`, `state_name`, `value_name`).
- `solar_charger_current_missing` carries the additive `integration` and `sensors`; `get_entity_config` carries the additive `detection.chargers` rows (applied with `apply_detection`) and `own_sensors` on a charger's `measured_current_source` field.

## Going back

- **Going back to 2.2 works out a target's need with the fixed efficiency again.** 2.2 does not read the learned energy per percent; it is kept and used again when you update.
- **Going back to 2.2 drops the two grid fee additions from every price, and keeps them stored.** 2.2 does not read them, so plans and prices are made without them; they are used again when you update. The two numbers and the hidden entities are left unavailable in 2.2 and can be removed there by hand.
