SpotNav for Home Assistant 2.3.0 plans on the power your car really draws, and plans again when a charge falls behind.

## New

- **A charge that falls behind is planned again within minutes.** The plan counts on the charger's current times its phases; a cold battery or a car that limits itself draws less and fell behind, and until now that was noticed only by chance, at the next hourly price check, a new reading of the car's level or the car ending the charge. SpotNav now compares once a minute, while a planned period runs, the energy delivered with what the plan expected by then. When the charge is 0.5 kWh or more behind on two checks a minute apart, the rest is planned again at once from what was delivered, and more quarter-hours are bought, later or right after the running period; at most once every five minutes. A period that delivered less than 80 % of its share is planned again just after it ends. A shortfall SpotNav made itself (load balancing, a home battery's band, a phase switch, the sun) is planned again only at the period's end. No new notice is sent for such a plan.
- **The plan counts on the power your car really draws.** SpotNav learns what the car draws at the plan's full current from the minutes of a planned period that nothing of SpotNav's held back, per car when the charger knows which car it charges, else per charger. After 20 such minutes over two plug-ins the plan counts on the lower of that and the current's power, so a car with a 7.4 kW onboard charger on an 11 kW charger gets enough quarter-hours from the start, and the card's plan, times and cost follow. The current sent to the charger never changes. A change of the phases, the voltage between phases or the charging current starts the learning again. There is no setting; *Download diagnostics* shows the learned power, what it rests on and the last check of a running period.

## Fixes

- **A home battery that takes what the car frees no longer gets the car stepped back above the fuse.** With *the battery gives way* on, a car lowered while the battery charged and filled the room it left was stepped straight back up, on a battery never seen to give way to the car, and the site read the fuse plus that step for one reading. The step back up now stays within the fuse, as every other raise does, until the battery has shown that it gives way.
- **A charge that starts as the home battery stops charging is no longer paused.** Right after a start the car is reckoned at what the site meter shows it took, since the charger's own reading lags. When the battery stopped charging in the same seconds, the site rose by much less than the car drew, the rest looked like house load, and the car was paused five seconds after its start. What the battery stopped drawing since the start now counts as the car's too.
- **A pause that comes while a start is still on its way holds.** When load balancing found no room for the car's minimum before the charger had answered a start, nothing was sent, the start landed and the car charged against the pause; the pause was then not known as load balancing's, so the charge was not resumed by itself and SpotNav said it had not started as planned. The pause now stops the start and keeps the charge waiting for room, and it is resumed as any balancing pause is.
- **A charge paused for load balancing is resumed after one minute, not three.** An OCPP charger sends one last meter value a few seconds after the stop, still showing the car's current, and nothing after it. That value held the resume back until it was two minutes old. It now counts as the charge before the pause.
- **A step up that the grid has room for is no longer taken back.** With *the battery gives way* on, a step up the battery did not make room for was taken back even when the site stayed within its limit. It is now taken back only when it took the site over the limit.

## Going back

- **Going back to 2.2 plans on the current's power again.** 2.2 does not read the learned power; it is kept and used again when you update.
