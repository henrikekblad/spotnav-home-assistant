SpotNav for Home Assistant 2.2.0 counts every charger's energy with the right meter, says what a plan really buys, plans on the power your car really draws, plans again when a charge falls behind and plans on Zonneplan's tariff in the Netherlands.

## New

- **Zonneplan's tariff in the Netherlands.** A new price area, *Netherlands – Zonneplan*, plans on Zonneplan's dynamic tariff as you pay it, read from the [Zonneplan](https://github.com/fsaris/home-assistant-zonneplan-one) integration (HACS); SpotNav does not contact Zonneplan itself. The tariff already holds Zonneplan's purchase fee, energy tax and VAT, so VAT and tax show as *Included in the price* and nothing is added; grid transfer stays a normal setting, off by default. The area *Netherlands* stays the spot price with your own fees, and the two never share days or a price history. While the Zonneplan integration is loaded and the area *Netherlands* has prices of its own, it shows a hint to choose *Netherlands – Zonneplan*; when the integration is down there is no fallback to the spot price, the card says what to do and the days already held keep planning.
- **The card says when the charger's own mode works against SpotNav, with a button to set it back.** Until now only the setup warned that, for example, Peblar's own smart charging was on, and the warning could be skipped; a Peblar on *Pure solar* then kept solar charging on three phases, since Peblar ignores its *Force single phase* switch unless smart charging is *Default*. The card's attention line and list now say *The charger's own smart charging is on (Pure solar). It works against SpotNav.* for as long as it is so, with the setting's name and values as Home Assistant words them in your language, and an administrator can press *Set to Default* to change that one setting; SpotNav checks first that it is still on and belongs to the charger. A charger whose own enable switch is off (Easee) gets a *Turn on* button beside the same notice. Nothing is changed without a press, and the item goes by itself once the setting is right.
- **A charge that falls behind is planned again within minutes.** The plan counts on the charger's current times its phases; a cold battery or a car that limits itself draws less and fell behind, and until now that was noticed only by chance, at the next hourly price check, a new reading of the car's level or the car ending the charge. SpotNav now compares once a minute, while a planned period runs, the energy delivered with what the plan expected by then. When the charge is 0.5 kWh or more behind on two checks a minute apart, the rest is planned again at once from what was delivered, and more quarter-hours are bought, later or right after the running period; at most once every five minutes. A period that delivered less than 80 % of its share is planned again just after it ends. A shortfall SpotNav made itself (load balancing, a home battery's band, a phase switch, the sun) is planned again only at the period's end. No new notice is sent for such a plan.
- **The plan counts on the power your car really draws.** SpotNav learns what the car draws at the plan's full current from the minutes of a planned period that nothing of SpotNav's held back, per car when the charger knows which car it charges, else per charger. After 20 such minutes over two plug-ins the plan counts on the lower of that and the current's power, so a car with a 7.4 kW onboard charger on an 11 kW charger gets enough quarter-hours from the start, and the card's plan, times and cost follow. The current sent to the charger never changes. A change of the phases, the voltage between phases or the charging current starts the learning again. There is no setting; *Download diagnostics* shows the learned power, what it rests on and the last check of a running period.

## Fixes

- **One charge is one entry in the charge history.** A charge SpotNav paused itself while the car stayed plugged in (load balancing, the sun waiting for surplus, a phase switch, or the gap between two windows of one plan) was split into one entry per stretch once a pause lasted over two minutes. It is now one entry from the first start to the last stop, with the energy and cost of every part; it ends when the car is unplugged, you stop the charge, the plan has charged what it needed or the car is full. Entries already recorded stay as they are.
- **A plan for a fixed amount now says the amount you asked for, and its cost.** A need SpotNav counts with the charger's energy meter ends the charge as soon as the meter shows it delivered, but the card stated the energy and cost of whole quarter-hours (30.5 kWh asked, 33.3 kWh and its cost shown). The plan now states 30.5 kWh, counts the last quarter-hour's cost only for the part the need takes, and the card's plan and its *Charging now; scheduled until* time end where the need is expected to be met. A charger without an energy meter is unchanged: nothing stops it early, so its whole quarter-hours are charged and shown.
- **Alfen (Modbus) counts all three phases.** SpotNav took the energy of phase L1 alone for the charger's meter, so a three-phase charge counted a third of what it delivered and a fixed amount ran on far past it, until the car was full or the departure came. SpotNav now reads *Real energy delivered sum*.
- **go-e Charger (legacy) and Fronius Wattpilot count with their lifetime meter.** SpotNav could take the charger's per-session counter for its meter (go-e's *Current Session charged*, Wattpilot's *Connection Charged*); it now takes the lifetime one (*Total Charged*, *Totally Charged*). Heidelberg Energy Control's per-session counter is now known as well, so it is never taken for the lifetime *Total energy*.
- **myenergi zappi, ABB Terra AC, Ohme, ChargePoint and Hypervolt count their energy from the charger's own power.** These integrations have no lifetime energy meter, and SpotNav took a per-session or daily counter (the zappi's *Energy used today*) or none. It now counts the energy from the charger's power sensor itself, as it does for a charger behind a smart plug, also when the integration's cloud reports only every few minutes; a gap in the readings is never filled with a guess. *Download diagnostics* says what each charger's energy is counted with.
- **A meter SpotNav chose wrongly is replaced when you update.** A charger added before 2.2.0 with one of those counters gets the right meter, or its power, at the first start, and the log says so. A need being counted at that moment, and the charge session being recorded, keep what they counted and go on from the new meter's first reading; whenever a charger's meter changes, two meters are never measured against each other. A separate energy meter of another integration is always kept. Before 2.2.0 SpotNav did not remember whether a meter was its suggestion or your choice: if you picked one of those counters on purpose, choose it again under *Energy* in the card, and from then on it is kept.
- **A changed charging current reaches the charge that runs, and your *Start*.** Lowering *Charging current* while a charge ran changed nothing on the charger until SpotNav made a new plan, which can wait for tomorrow's prices; a *Stop* and *Start* meanwhile started at the old plan's current again. The current you set is now written to a charge SpotNav runs (a plan's, a top-off, yours, *Charge now*) when you change it, never above what load balancing allows, and no faster than the charger takes writes: a charger written by cloud gets it within a minute or two. A charger that keeps its current in flash memory gets it at the next start. Every start, yours and the plan's, starts at it. Solar charging still sets its own current, never above this one.
- **A planned period no longer takes over a charge you started.** When a planned period began while a charge you had started (*Start*, or *Charge now*) was running, SpotNav could take that charge over as the plan's, and the period's end then stopped it. The charge now stays yours, as the guide says: nothing is sent when the period begins, its end does not stop the charge, and the energy it delivers still counts toward the need.
- **A restart reads who owns a charge from one saved record.** SpotNav now saves who started a charge, and what load balancing or a hold keeps back, in one record written together with the rest of the charger's state, and a restart reads that record alone. A restart in the moment a start or a stop goes out keeps the charge as that command makes it, and a charge started by hand stays yours after a restart even when the planned period is open.
- **Every charger uses the current charging logic; the choice of the previous logic is gone.** Until now *Configure → Charging decisions* could set one charger back to the previous logic. That logic is now removed, and so is the choice. A charger that was set to *Previous logic* uses the current logic after the update, keeps a charge that is running, and the log says so once. When a charging decision cannot be taken, SpotNav no longer hands it to the previous logic: it starts or stops nothing automatically at that moment and decides again at the next one, while your own *Start* and *Stop* still work and load balancing still protects the fuse. The Repairs message, shown only when that repeats, says so.

- **Load balancing protects the fuse on a site whose meter does not say import from export.** On a site that reads a current per phase without *The grid current is signed* and the total grid power (a Tibber Pulse, AMS HAN, Ferroamp or frient meter set up by hand, or any current sensors you picked), load balancing never knew which way a phase flowed and did nothing, while the card showed it on: a reported phase read 32.8 A against a 25 A fuse for minutes. It now lowers the car at once when a phase it draws on is over the limit, whoever started the charge, pauses it where its minimum does not fit and starts it again when there is room; it never raises it there. The load-balancing row in the card's settings says that it only protects the fuse there and what to set: turn on *Grid current is signed* and choose *Total grid power* under *Site entities* (no longer labelled "for solar": load balancing needs it too), or press *Use* on your meter under *Found in Home Assistant*. Then load balancing works fully, and the line goes.
- **Load balancing lowers a car it has just raised instead of pausing it.** An OCPP charger reports its current about half a minute apart, so right after a raise its reading still shows the old current while the site meter already shows the car at the new one. A load that came on in that half minute (a washing machine's heater) was reckoned on top of the old current, and the car was paused where lowering it would have done. Until the charger's own reading catches up, the car is now reckoned at what the site meter shows it took, never more than was written: it is lowered to what fits, and paused only where its minimum does not fit.

## Going back

- **Going back to 2.1 keeps a need's count and a running charge's session; going back to 2.0 does not.** 2.2.0 notes which energy meter a count reads; 2.0 does not know that note and drops the record, so a fixed amount being counted is planned in full again and a charge in progress is not recorded.
- **Going back to 2.1 reads who owns a charge as 2.2 left it.** 2.2 still writes the keys 2.1 reads beside its own record, so a charge, a hold and what load balancing keeps back are read the same. A charger that was set to *Previous logic* uses the current logic in 2.1 too, as 2.1 does by default.
- **Going back to 2.1 plans on the current's power again.** 2.1 does not read the learned power; it is kept and used again when you update.
