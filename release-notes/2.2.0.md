SpotNav for Home Assistant 2.2.0 counts every charger's energy with the right meter, says what a plan really buys and plans on Zonneplan's tariff in the Netherlands.

## New

- **Zonneplan's tariff in the Netherlands.** A new price area, *Netherlands – Zonneplan*, plans on Zonneplan's dynamic tariff as you pay it, read from the [Zonneplan](https://github.com/fsaris/home-assistant-zonneplan-one) integration (HACS); SpotNav does not contact Zonneplan itself. The tariff already holds Zonneplan's purchase fee, energy tax and VAT, so VAT and tax show as *Included in the price* and nothing is added; grid transfer stays a normal setting, off by default. The area *Netherlands* stays the spot price with your own fees, and the two never share days or a price history. While the Zonneplan integration is loaded and the area *Netherlands* has prices of its own, it shows a hint to choose *Netherlands – Zonneplan*; when the integration is down there is no fallback to the spot price, the card says what to do and the days already held keep planning.

## Fixes

- **A plan for a fixed amount now says the amount you asked for, and its cost.** A need SpotNav counts with the charger's energy meter ends the charge as soon as the meter shows it delivered, but the card stated the energy and cost of whole quarter-hours (30.5 kWh asked, 33.3 kWh and its cost shown). The plan now states 30.5 kWh, counts the last quarter-hour's cost only for the part the need takes, and the card's plan and its *Charging now; scheduled until* time end where the need is expected to be met. A charger without an energy meter is unchanged: nothing stops it early, so its whole quarter-hours are charged and shown.
- **Alfen (Modbus) counts all three phases.** SpotNav took the energy of phase L1 alone for the charger's meter, so a three-phase charge counted a third of what it delivered and a fixed amount ran on far past it, until the car was full or the departure came. SpotNav now reads *Real energy delivered sum*.
- **go-e Charger (legacy) and Fronius Wattpilot count with their lifetime meter.** SpotNav could take the charger's per-session counter for its meter (go-e's *Current Session charged*, Wattpilot's *Connection Charged*); it now takes the lifetime one (*Total Charged*, *Totally Charged*). Heidelberg Energy Control's per-session counter is now known as well, so it is never taken for the lifetime *Total energy*.
- **myenergi zappi, ABB Terra AC, Ohme, ChargePoint and Hypervolt count their energy from the charger's own power.** These integrations have no lifetime energy meter, and SpotNav took a per-session or daily counter (the zappi's *Energy used today*) or none. It now counts the energy from the charger's power sensor itself, as it does for a charger behind a smart plug, also when the integration's cloud reports only every few minutes; a gap in the readings is never filled with a guess. *Download diagnostics* says what each charger's energy is counted with.
- **A meter SpotNav chose wrongly is replaced when you update.** A charger added before 2.2.0 with one of those counters gets the right meter, or its power, at the first start, and the log says so. A need being counted at that moment, and the charge session being recorded, keep what they counted and go on from the new meter's first reading; whenever a charger's meter changes, two meters are never measured against each other. A separate energy meter of another integration is always kept. Before 2.2.0 SpotNav did not remember whether a meter was its suggestion or your choice: if you picked one of those counters on purpose, choose it again under *Energy* in the card, and from then on it is kept.

- **Load balancing lowers a car it has just raised instead of pausing it.** An OCPP charger reports its current about half a minute apart, so right after a raise its reading still shows the old current while the site meter already shows the car at the new one. A load that came on in that half minute (a washing machine's heater) was reckoned on top of the old current, and the car was paused where lowering it would have done. Until the charger's own reading catches up, the car is now reckoned at what the site meter shows it took, never more than was written: it is lowered to what fits, and paused only where its minimum does not fit.

## Going back

- **Going back to 2.1 keeps a need's count and a running charge's session; going back to 2.0 does not.** 2.2.0 notes which energy meter a count reads; 2.0 does not know that note and drops the record, so a fixed amount being counted is planned in full again and a charge in progress is not recorded.
