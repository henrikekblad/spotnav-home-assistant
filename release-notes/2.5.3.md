SpotNav for Home Assistant 2.5.3 stops warning that the car will not be ready when the plan falls short by only a sliver.

## New

- **A blueprint for a heat pump's relay: Relay by electricity price.** A relay (a Shelly or any switch) on a heat pump's potential-free input is closed while electricity is expensive (heating curve lowered or an add-on heater blocked, with a longest closed time, a rest after it and an outdoor temperature floor), pulsed when it is cheap (extra hot water, at most a set number of times a day), or drives the two SG Ready inputs. SpotNav installs it with the other two, under the card's Settings, Support, Automations.

## Fixes

- **Relay by electricity price no longer closes the relay for a single expensive quarter-hour.** A new input, Shortest closing (45 minutes, 0 turns it off), closes the relay only when the expensive stretch ahead lasts at least that long; once closed it stays closed until Expensive now ends.
- **No "will not be ready by the departure" when the plan holds all but a sliver of the need.** A plan is made of whole quarter-hours; when the last one needed would start just after the departure, the plan said "Will not be ready by the departure: about 40.2 of 40.2 kWh by 13:00" although the car would be done by about 12:40. A plan that falls short by at most one percent of the need now counts as ready; every quarter-hour up to the departure is still planned, and a real shortfall is still told as before.
- **A charger's settings stored by a later SpotNav with a departure calendar now load in this release.** Going back from a 3.0 beta kept the charger's area, current, departure and target at their defaults until saved again; now the record loads and only the calendar is left unused.
- **No more charge-limit question about a presence sensor, and the question can be dismissed.** An mmWave presence sensor reports a distance and percent settings, which looked like a car's range and two charge limits, so Repairs asked "A vehicle's charge limit needs a decision" about it and offered no way out. A charge limit is now asked about only on a device that also reports a state of charge, the leftover question goes away by itself, and the charge-limit question now offers "This is not a vehicle" like the state-of-charge one.
