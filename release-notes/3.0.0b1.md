SpotNav for Home Assistant 3.0.0b1 is the first public beta of 3.0. **Learns your home. Plans your energy.**

With 3.0 SpotNav becomes a home energy management system (HEMS) inside Home Assistant: besides your car it now plans the house, the home battery, the heat pump and the hot water on the same prices, the same forecasts and the same main fuse. Everything still runs on your own Home Assistant, with no account and no cloud service of SpotNav's own.

**This is a beta, offered only to those who switch on beta versions in HACS.** 2.5.x stays the normal release and keeps getting its fixes. Car charging and load balancing act as before. Every new part that could write to a battery or a heat pump runs in **shadow** first: it plans, shows what it would do and what that would have saved, and writes nothing until you turn it on and a check on your own hardware has passed.

## What is new

- **Three cards, one bundle.** The card picker now lists **SpotNav** (charging, as before), **SpotNav house** and **SpotNav heating**. Each house or heating card takes `site:` (or `charger:` to use that charger's home). All three share one settings popover, where every setting lives, with the site-wide ones marked.
- **The house card.** The whole house on one card: live flows between the sun, the grid, the battery, the house, the car and the heat; the energy history by day, month and year; what the battery earned and what SpotNav's plan would have saved; the battery plan; and the price. Its header says "· Test" while SpotNav controls nothing, "· Own control" while the battery's own optimiser runs it (Sigenergy's AI mode, for one), and what the battery does once SpotNav controls it. The card itself never writes to anything. See [The house card](docs/house-card.md).
- **Controls that cannot act are left out.** Where nothing could happen, the cards and the app say so instead of offering a control: no battery found, no battery settings; no heating found, the heating card is one "nothing found" row; a heat pump SpotNav can only read has no −/+ and no Home/Away, only its target; no solar, no Sol and Hybrid for household members (administrators keep them with the set-up link); house tabs that can never fill, and the import target where the site cannot measure it, are left out. Settings that turn a feature on, and safety statuses, always stay.
- **A house load forecast.** SpotNav learns what your house draws, hour by hour, and forecasts the next 48 hours: six weeks of measured hours, weekdays and weekends apart, public holidays where Home Assistant's Holiday integration gives a calendar, and the outdoor temperature where it really explains your load. With a home battery or solar production it forecasts the house's whole load, so the battery's own cycling is not taken for the house. Under an import target the car's plans use it to plan around the evening peak. It is shown as the site's *House load forecast* sensor. See [Forecasts](docs/forecasts.md).
- **A battery plan.** For a battery SpotNav can read (Sigenergy, Huawei Solar, SolarEdge Modbus Multi, Victron, GoodWe, SolaX Modbus, Enphase, and the Sofar, Growatt and Solinteg plugins of SolaX Modbus; some learn their size or ratings first), SpotNav plans the next day or two: when to charge from the grid, when to hold, when to cover the house. It reads the prices with your fees, the house forecast, the corrected sun and your cars' plans. Choose the **Mode** in the settings' Home battery section: **Self-consumption**, **Balanced** (the default) or **Max profit**. Balanced sells from the battery only when it is clearly profitable: the house comes first, and only the rest is sold, and only where a kWh earns at least a margin (1 kr in Sweden, a similar sum per currency) more than keeping it is worth; Max profit sells whenever it pays; Self-consumption never sells from the battery. The wear cost and Balanced's margin are shown read-only under the Mode. Each night SpotNav reviews what each mode would have saved against what the battery really did; with Sigenergy's AI mode that is a benchmark against it. See [The battery plan](docs/battery-plan.md).
- **Battery control, opt-in and in testing.** The **Battery control** switch in the Home battery section lets SpotNav run the plan on the battery: hold it while the car charges from the grid, charge it in cheap hours, cover the house, and stop export when an exported kWh would cost money. Switching it on starts a check, not control: hold, a 2 kW grid charge, cover the house, hand back, each written, read back and judged, about 40 minutes at a suitable evening moment. Control starts only once that check has passed, and runs under fixed safety rules: every write read back, a grid charge capped so that no phase passes the main fuse even if Home Assistant stops in the middle of it, at most 96 writes a day to the plant's settings, and the battery handed back as found whenever something is unexpected. Sigenergy is the brand this control has run on, on one site. In 3.0.0b1 no brand is yet marked field-tested, Sigenergy included, so outside beta tester mode the switch only logs what control would do ("Test: SpotNav controls nothing yet and only logs what it would do"). Huawei, SolarEdge, SolaX, GoodWe, Tesla Powerwall, Solinteg, Ferroamp and Victron are mapped from their integrations and have never run on a real device: they run only for beta testers, and only after their own check has passed on that tester's plant. See [Home batteries](docs/home-battery.md).
- **Grid charging per phase.** A house that loads one phase leaves a balanced grid charge almost nothing under the fuse. On a plant that takes a power target per phase (Sigenergy with independent phase power control; Victron), SpotNav can give each phase its own cap from that phase's learned peak, after an extra check step that shows the plant's phase A really is your meter's L1. The cap learns the house's peaks per phase over one to two weeks; until then it uses a cautious rule (4 kW on one phase), which on a 20 A fuse leaves no grid charge at all. Solinteg has the commands but cannot use them yet: its integration does not report its power per phase.
- **Heat control paths.** The **SpotNav heating** card shows the wanted temperature, **Home** or **Away**, the coldest room and the day's heat plan, which lowers the heat in expensive hours within fixed comfort rules (at most 3 hours in a row and 6 hours a day, never at −15 °C or colder, released at once when a room falls below the comfort band). **Control the heat**, off by default, lets SpotNav steer through one path you choose: a **relay** on the pump's lowering input, **SG Ready**'s two contacts (which can also raise the heat before an expensive run, and never use forced raise), or, through the pump's own integration, **SG Ready on a NIBE S series** or the **heating curve offset** (NIBE F and S series, CTC). A short check runs first. SpotNav never writes a setpoint, and a change made by someone else wins. The paths through the pump's own integration have not yet run on a real pump. See [Heat pumps](docs/heat-pumps.md).
- **Hot water planning, in shadow.** SpotNav learns when your household uses hot water (the peaks, such as the morning showers) from the heat pump meter and plans one tank reheat into the cheap hours before each peak. It also keeps the heat plan's lowerings away from the pump's own reheats before a peak. Both start in shadow and go live only under **Control the heat**, each behind its own gate: planned runs after at least 7 days with every weekday seen, 10 peaks, 70 % of them hit and a saving; the blocks around hot water after at least 14 days, 10 peaks and 70 % hit. The pump's own legionella programme is never turned off. See [Hot water](docs/hot-water.md).
- **Hot water now.** Where the pump has a hot-water boost that ends by itself (NIBE's temporary lux, myUplink's temporary lux, CTC's extra hot water) or you chose a relay on the pump's hot-water input, the heating card's Now tab has a **Hot water now** button (the app calls it Boost). It works from the start, for anyone who sees the card. It is your own start: SpotNav never stops it and never sheds it for the fuse.
- **SpotNav's own solar forecast, in the background.** On a site with solar production in the Energy dashboard, SpotNav learns your panels' direction, tilt and low-sun shade from their own history, reads an irradiance forecast from Open-Meteo, and scores its forecast each night beside the one in use. It feeds no plan yet. The correction of your existing forecast (a factor for clear hours and one for cloudy hours, *Solar forecast* sensor) is used by the battery plan once it has learned; the Hybrid strategy still reads the raw forecast. See [Solar](docs/solar.md).
- **Beta tester mode and the hardware test.** **Settings → Support → Beta tester** (administrators, off by default) lets control that otherwise waits for SpotNav to learn your home start at once, and lets a brand not yet tested on a real plant leave test mode so its check can run. It never skips the fuse limits, the daily write limits, the brand's read-back check, your acknowledgements (such as taking over Sigenergy's AI mode) or the heating's safety rules. **Run hardware test**, in the same dialog and for any administrator, tests the battery and the heat path the way SpotNav controls them and hands everything back: at most ten minutes, at most a few kronor of grid energy, started only by you. See [Beta testing](docs/beta-testing.md).
- **The app works without a charger.** Pairing now hands the app every home as well as every charger, each with its own private webhook, so the app connects to the home through Home Assistant: a home with a battery or a heat pump and **no charger** can use it too, and a charger added to the home later shows up by itself. This needs the SpotNav app 3.0; app 2.4 sees the chargers as before.
- **Disconnect all phones.** In any card's **Settings → Support** an administrator can give every charger and home a new webhook id at once: every paired phone stops reaching Home Assistant and has to be paired again. Charging and heating go on.
- **Make a home of this.** A charger whose installation has no home yet gets **Make a home of this** in its Settings: it asks for the main fuse and makes a home around the charger, so the house, battery and heat pages can follow. Load balancing stays off until you choose a meter in the home's settings.
- **Also new:** the supplier's markup and what a sold kWh earns on the electricity price (both off until you set them; the markup is part of every buying price SpotNav plans with); a departure taken from a Home Assistant calendar in a charger's Plan dialog; an *Expected solar surplus* sensor for your own automations; a switched load (a water heater on a relay) planned in shadow; and the action **SpotNav: Export a day's records** (`spotnav.export_day`), which writes a day's forecasts, prices, battery plan and review to a JSON file in your configuration folder.

## What acts and what only watches

| Part | In 3.0.0b1 |
| --- | --- |
| EV charging, load balancing, the "charging from the grid" signals | **Acts**, as before |
| Calendar departure, supplier markup | **Acts** on the car's plans |
| House load forecast | **Acts** on car plans under an import target; otherwise shown |
| House card | **Shows only**; it writes to nothing |
| Battery plan, the three modes, the nightly review | **Shadow**: computed and shown |
| Battery control (plan, hold for the car, grid charge per phase, no export at a negative price) | **Off by default.** Switched on: only logs what it would do, for every brand. For a beta tester: **acts** once the brand's check has passed on that plant |
| Heat control (relay, SG Ready, NIBE SG Ready, curve offset) | **Shadow by default.** **Acts** with *Control the heat* on and its check passed |
| Hot water: planned runs and blocks around hot water | **Shadow** until *Control the heat* is on and its gate has opened (a beta tester skips the shadow days, never a safety rule) |
| Hot water now button | **Acts** on your press, from the start |
| Solar forecast correction | **Background**; read by the battery plan, not by Hybrid charging |
| SpotNav's own solar forecast | **Background only**: logged and scored, used by no plan |
| Switched load | **Shadow**: shown, never switched |
| Expected solar surplus sensor | Read-only; SpotNav acts on nothing from it |

When SpotNav stops controlling something, it puts back what it found. It never takes over a device another controller runs (Predbat, EMHASS, CheckWatt, EffektGuard, the pump's own price control): it watches and says so.

## What changes for 2.x users

- **Nothing to set up.** Your chargers, sites, prices and plans carry over. The forecasts and the battery plan start learning from the hours SpotNav already measures; the house card says what each part is still waiting for. A first useful house forecast needs a few days, the first nightly review comes the night after the first whole day, and the solar correction needs at least three days with sun.
- **The app moves to the home by itself.** A phone paired before 3.0 holds one webhook per charger. With app 3.0 it asks each charger once for its home and moves to the home's webhook silently, with no re-pairing and no question. App 2.4 is not affected.
- **Home is listed first** when you add to SpotNav, worded as "Your home (house, battery, heat, load balancing)" rather than a load-balancing group. A home is still optional; a charger works without one.
- **The house and heating cards lost "(preview)"** in the card picker.
- **Leftovers removed.** The selling switch of early 3.0 test builds is gone: the battery's **Mode** now decides selling, and a site that had allowed selling and never chose a mode is set to Max profit once. Some old 2.x bookkeeping keys are read once and no longer written. You do not need to do anything.

## How to get the beta

1. **Make a Home Assistant backup first** (Settings → System → Backups), and note which SpotNav version you run now.
2. In HACS, open SpotNav, then the menu (three dots), *Redownload*. Switch on *Show beta versions* and choose `3.0.0b1`. If you only ever press *Update*, you stay on 2.5.x: HACS offers a pre-release only to those who switch this on.
3. Restart Home Assistant, then hard-refresh the browser (or restart the app) so the new cards load.
4. Optional: add the **SpotNav house** and **SpotNav heating** cards from the card picker.

## Going back to 2.5.x

Before you go back, **turn off Battery control and Control the heat** and wait until their lines say off, so the battery and the heat pump are handed back while 3.0 is still there to do it. 3.0 also hands back when Home Assistant stops, but only for a bounded time; whatever is left is finished at its next start, which 2.5.x does not do.

Then *Redownload* in HACS, choose **2.5.5 or later**, and restart. 2.5.5 (released) is the safe way back: it reads everything 3.0 stores and keeps 3.0's charge sessions and records. 2.5.4 or older refuses the charge sessions recorded under 3.0 and drops some of its hourly records, so do not go back to those.

- Your chargers, sites, prices and charge sessions load in 2.5.5 and later as before. The new data of 3.0 (forecasts, battery plan, heat and hot water, beta tester) is kept on disk and simply not read.
- A departure calendar is kept unused in 2.5.3 and later, and still there when you install 3.0 again. In 2.5.2 and earlier such a charger's settings run at their defaults until saved again.
- The supplier markup and the sold-kWh figure stay stored, but 2.5.x does not use them, so its plans are priced without the markup.
- The home's webhook closes, so an app 3.0 phone that uses it asks to pair again; pairing then hands over the chargers only.
- The house and heating cards do not exist in 2.5.x: remove them from your dashboards. The 3.0 sensors (*House load forecast*, *Solar forecast*, *Expected solar surplus*) show as no longer provided and can be deleted.

If you restore the backup instead, you get back exactly what you had.

## Known limits

- It is a beta: expect rough edges, in the house and heating cards above all. Tell us on GitHub, or send debug info from the card's **Settings → Support**.
- Battery control acts only for beta testers in this release, and only after the brand's check has passed on their plant. Every brand but Sigenergy is untested on a real device; GoodWe and Tesla Powerwall (slow, through Tesla's cloud) are among them. Sofar and Growatt are read only.
- Sigenergy, Solinteg, GoodWe, Ferroamp and Tesla have no dead-man: if Home Assistant stops during a command, the plant keeps it. SpotNav caps a grid charge for that case, and on a small main fuse that can mean no grid charge at all until the house's peaks are learned.
- The heat paths through the pump's own integration (NIBE SG Ready, curve offset) have not yet run on a real pump. Through NIBE's cloud (myUplink) nothing is steered.
- The battery plan needs the battery's level, usable size and rated power; where the integration lacks one, it is learned first, and some batteries need several charges before the plan starts.
- The battery plan's review counts the battery as ideal (no real losses or ramp limits), so a plan looks a few percent better than it would be.
- SpotNav's own solar forecast feeds no plan, and the Hybrid strategy still reads the raw solar forecast.
- A home without a charger needs app 3.0 to show anything in the app; it gets no phone notifications.

## Privacy

SpotNav sends nothing about your installation unless you ask it to.

- **Beta tester** is opt-in, administrators only, and off by default. While it is ticked, SpotNav sends the same redacted file that *Send debug info* sends, marked as a beta report, once a night for 14 days; then the box unticks itself and nothing more is sent. Untick it at any time and sending stops. A report that fails is skipped, not sent later. The file holds versions, settings, entity names and recent logs; never passwords, tokens, e-mail addresses or your exact location. An e-mail address is optional and used only to tell you about a problem seen in your reports or a fix for it. Beta reports and the address are kept 90 days, then deleted.
- **The hardware test** result goes into the debug info; with the beta box ticked it is sent by itself, otherwise the dialog offers **Send**.
- **SpotNav's own solar forecast** sends only your home position rounded to 0.1° (about 10 km) to Open-Meteo, and only on a site with solar production.
- **App pairing** stores no Home Assistant password or token on the phone, only the private webhooks of your homes and chargers.

See [Privacy](docs/privacy.md) and the [privacy policy](https://spotnav.sensnology.se/privacy).
