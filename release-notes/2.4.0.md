SpotNav for Home Assistant 2.4.0 measures a demand charge on the site's meter, part by part, and holds the site's chargers under an optional import target, so each clock hour's mean import stays under it. A demand charge is measured and shown only: nothing holds the charging for it yet.

## New

- **A demand charge, measured and shown.** Some network operators charge per kW of the month's highest hourly mean of import. The electricity price can now hold up to three parts of such a charge, each counting all hours or only certain hours (set like the high-load hours, across midnight if needed), the highest hour or the mean of the 3 or 5 highest hours on different days, and an optional price per kW for November to March and for April to October. Linköping's alternative tariff is two parts (06–23 and 23–06, one peak each); Göteborg's standard tariff is one (all hours, 3 days). In the card's Settings the price section shows *Demand charge: None* until you add a part; then each part with this month's figure and its peaks, for example *This month: 7.0 kW – Tue 22 Sep 09:00 · 7.0 kW*. SpotNav does not change any plan or charge for it in this version.
- **Each hour's import is measured on the site.** A site with signed grid power (a derived site, or a direct site with its total grid power) measures every clock hour's mean net import, on the area's clock, keeping the month's hours across restarts. An hour with more than ten minutes without a reading is shown but never sets a peak. "Get started" asks for the site's total grid power while a demand charge or an import target is set and the site cannot measure.
- **An import target that holds the chargers.** Settings has *Import target* beside the main fuse, off until set: the hourly mean of import, in kW, you want to stay under. While set, a line under it shows the hour running now against it, *This hour: 4.2 of 6.0 kW*. With load balancing on, each charger is given the smaller of what the main fuse allows and what the hour's budget leaves it: the target less what was imported so far, spread over the time left, less what the house draws now. The fuse's rules come first and are never loosened. A car is never held below its minimum current; once the hour can no longer end under the target with it at its minimum, it is paused until the next hour (once per hour) and resumed then. Nothing is raised in an hour's last ten minutes. The status says *Held to 7 A to keep this hour under 6.0 kW (now 5.1 kW)* or *Paused until 15:00 to keep this hour under 6.0 kW*. Plans count on the power the target leaves the car, so they buy the hours the held charge needs. Below the car's minimum level SpotNav charges at once, never held by the target. Above it, when the plan cannot be ready by its departure under the target, the plan's new *If the car cannot be ready in time* (in the Plan dialog while the site has a target) decides: *Ask* (the default) holds the target and tells your phones once, *The car will not be ready by 07:00 under the 6.0 kW import target – 4 kWh short*, with a *Release tonight* button (the card shows the same press); *Release the target* lets the departure win by itself; *Hold the target* holds it and only says what will be missing. *Release tonight* sets the target aside for that charge until its departure or an unplug, and a restart keeps it. A house that alone is above the target pauses the chargers and nothing more, and the status says the house alone is above it. A person's Start is not held. Under *battery first* a discharging home battery counts as import. Without a live meter reading, with more than ten minutes of the hour missing, or with load balancing off, nothing is held for the target.
- **A Demand-charge peak sensor on the site** (kW) while its electricity price has a part: the first part's figure this month, with every part's figure and peaks and the running hour as attributes. The site's diagnostics carry a `demand` block with every hour of the month.

## For app and card developers

- Five status lines for the import target: `import_target_held`, `import_target_paused`, `import_target_at_risk`, `import_target_released` and `import_target_house_over`, and the load-balancing cause `import_target`. `spotnav/release_import_target` and the webhook's `release_import_target` set the target aside for a charge (capability `release_import_target`). The settings record carries `if_not_ready` (withheld from older apps, and kept by their writes). The webhook gives an app that does not read `import_target_status` the load-balancing lines it already words in their place. The site's diagnostics carry an `import_target` block.
- The `grid_tariff` block carries `demand` (the parts, what the site measured for them, the running hour and the import target), and `update_grid_tariff` takes an optional `demand_components`; a write without it keeps the stored parts. `get_entity_config` lists the site field `import_target_kw`, written without reloading the site. "Get started" has a new row, `demand_grid_power`. `api_version` stays 1. See [Apps and API](../docs/api.md).

## Going back

- **Going back to 2.3 drops the demand charge and the import target from view, and keeps them stored, and nothing holds the chargers for the target any more.** A plan whose *If the car cannot be ready in time* was set to *Release the target* or *Hold the target* stores a field 2.3 does not know: set it back to *Ask* before going back, or 2.3 starts that charger's plan settings over. 2.3 does not read them; they are shown again when you update. The Demand-charge peak sensor is left unavailable in 2.3 and can be removed there by hand. The hours measured while on 2.3 are missing.
