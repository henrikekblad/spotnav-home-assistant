SpotNav for Home Assistant 2.0.0 changes where the electricity prices come from. Please read *What to do when you upgrade* before you update. It also brings the fixes and the automation change listed at the end.


## Prices from your own Home Assistant

Day-ahead prices belong to the power exchanges, and it is unclear whether a free service may pass them on to its users. To be on the safe side, SpotNav no longer delivers prices from its own server. Your Home Assistant now fetches them itself: through Home Assistant's built-in Nord Pool integration, the PVPC integration in Spain, the Octopus Energy integration for Agile, or your own free ENTSO-E key.

- **Which source an area uses follows from what you have added.** There is no setting to choose it: the PVPC integration for Spain's regulated PVPC, the Octopus Energy integration for an Agile region in Great Britain, Nord Pool for an area it trades while the Nord Pool integration is added, and otherwise your ENTSO-E key. The card's **Settings → Electricity price** names the source: *Prices from Nord Pool (via Home Assistant)*, *Prices from ENTSO-E (your key)* and so on.
- **When an area has no source, the card says what to add,** with a button that opens Home Assistant's own dialog for it, and the electricity price names it as its problem. The prices SpotNav already holds keep showing. Once there is nothing priced for today, Home Assistant raises a Repairs issue, *No electricity prices for …*, which goes away by itself when prices are back.
- **One ENTSO-E key for the whole installation,** entered in the card's **Settings → Electricity price** or under the electricity price's **Configure**. It is free: register at transparency.entsoe.eu, then e-mail transparency@entsoe.eu with the subject "Restful API access" and your account's e-mail address. Once access is granted (it can take a few days), generate the security token under **My Account Settings**. The key is stored in Home Assistant, never logged and never shown in diagnostics.
- **The history profile is built in Home Assistant** from the prices it has stored, and the missing past days are filled from your source in the background, so planning a departure days ahead keeps working.
- **What SpotNav contacts for prices** is now only what you added (Home Assistant's Nord Pool, PVPC or Octopus Energy integration, which contact their own services), the ENTSO-E platform when you have entered a key, and the European Central Bank's public exchange rates once a day for an area priced in another currency than the euro. The SpotNav server is still used for one thing: waking the SpotNav app for its notifications, as before.

## What to do when you upgrade

Nothing breaks on the upgrade: your settings, your charge history and the prices already stored stay as they are. But without a source, an area gets no new prices once the stored ones run out, usually by the next day. So, right after updating, open the card's **Settings → Electricity price** and do what the notice there says:

- **Sweden, Norway, Denmark, Finland, Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania, Germany/Luxembourg, the Netherlands, Belgium, France, Austria, Poland, Romania, Bulgaria:** add Home Assistant's built-in **Nord Pool** integration (**Add Nord Pool** in the card, or **Settings → Devices & services → Add integration → Nord Pool**). The area and currency you pick when adding it do not matter: SpotNav asks for the area it needs. If you already have the Nord Pool integration, there is nothing to do. Or enter your own ENTSO-E key instead.
- **Spain – PVPC (regulated):** add Home Assistant's built-in **PVPC** integration (*Spain electricity hourly pricing (PVPC)*).
- **Great Britain – Octopus Agile:** install the **Octopus Energy** integration from HACS and add it with your account. SpotNav reads your Agile meter's rates from it, so they are your own tariff's prices.
- **Every other area** (Switzerland, Czechia, Slovakia, Hungary, Slovenia, Croatia, Greece, Italy, Spain day-ahead, Portugal): enter your own free **ENTSO-E key**, as described above.

The [Prices](https://github.com/henrikekblad/spotnav-home-assistant/blob/main/docs/prices.md#where-prices-come-from) page and [Troubleshooting](https://github.com/henrikekblad/spotnav-home-assistant/blob/main/docs/troubleshooting.md#prices-are-missing) have the details.

## The SpotNav app

A paired SpotNav app reads its prices from your Home Assistant, so it shows the same prices, from the same source, as the card. This needs SpotNav app 2.0.0 or later, rolling out on Google Play. Without Home Assistant, the app now fetches prices from ENTSO-E with your own key, and shows how to get one.

## Fixes

- **A silent meter no longer leaves the car at full current.** With load balancing on, a site meter that has stopped reporting (stale or unavailable) used to leave every charger at the current it had, for as long as the meter was gone. After a minute without a usable reading, load balancing now sets each charger it governs to its minimum current, and the card says so; the charge is not stopped. Once the meter reads again, the car climbs back by load balancing's usual steps.
- **A meter that drops out for a few seconds no longer shows on the card.** A Shelly Pro 3EM, among others, reads unavailable for some seconds each time it reconnects; the card now names a missing or stale site meter only once it has been gone for a minute, as long as load balancing waits before it acts, and the line goes as soon as the meter reads again.
- **A charge held back by load balancing stays held after a restart.** A restart of Home Assistant inside an open plan window no longer starts the plan's charge that load balancing had paused for want of room: it stays paused, and load balancing gives it back when there is room again, as without the restart.
- **A charge held back by load balancing goes on when load balancing ends.** Turning load balancing off, taking a charger off the site or removing the site no longer leaves a charge that load balancing had paused stopped for the rest of the window: it goes on at once, unless Auto was paused meanwhile.
- **Phase switching on NRGkick and Fronius Wattpilot happens only before a solar start.** A switch while charging faulted a car on an NRGkick and can leave a Wattpilot paused, so a charge that runs on either now follows the solar rules without switching, as on Easee.
- **An AMS HAN reader on an Aidon meter is read while a phase exports.** Aidon meters send a phase's current as negative while it exports; SpotNav now reads its size instead of taking the measurement as invalid, which stopped load balancing and solar.
- **ABB Terra AC gets its current changes at least 30 seconds apart.** Changes closer than about 15 seconds can freeze or reset the charger. Its stop and start count toward the 30 seconds but are never delayed by them.
- **A reading far beyond the main fuse is taken as a meter fault.** A grid current over twice the main fuse, or a phase or total power over what twice the fuse carries, is now invalid rather than a load, as when an integration reads kilowatts as watts. A real overload stays far below that bound.
- **An ABB Terra AC that starts by itself while paused is paused again.** SpotNav now reads an ABB's charge on its current limit, not on its start button, which reads unknown until it is first pressed: a firmware reset that put the limit back at 32 A outside the plan's periods went unnoticed.

## Automations

- **A finished charge says what it cost.** The Charger events entity's `charge_finished` now carries the charge's `cost`, `currency` and `average_price` per kWh, priced as the last-charge sensors are, and `unpriced_kwh` for energy with no published price, so a notification can say "12.4 kWh for 18.60 SEK, average 1.50 SEK/kWh". With nothing priced the cost is empty, never zero.
