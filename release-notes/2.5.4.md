SpotNav for Home Assistant 2.5.4 keeps a charge that load balancing paused from being forgotten, so it starts again as soon as the site has room.

## Fixes

- **A charge paused by load balancing at its window's start is no longer left off for the rest of the window.** A home battery began charging from the grid in the minute the plan's window opened; the car was cut, then paused. The charger kept charging for a moment, so the pause was sent a second time, and while that stop was on its way the charger reported its charge once more. SpotNav took that report as the plan's charge, which is right, but then counted it as a new start that ends the pause, which it is not: nothing is sent to the charger for it. The pause was forgotten, the charger was off with nothing waiting to resume it, and the car stood until the next window started it an hour and a half later, the last half hour and more of that with room for 19 A. Such a report now leaves the pause in place, and the charge is resumed as soon as the site has room for the charger's minimum.
- **A charge that load balancing paused is looked at again a minute and a half later.** If the charger is then off in an open window of the plan with no pause on record and nothing else holding it (a person's Stop or Start, a reached target, the car having ended the charge, the sun owning the charger), the window's start is decided again, at most three times, as a start that did not take is, and only once per window. A charger that still reads on at that look is looked at once more. Never after a safety stop (a current the charger did not take when it had to be lowered): starting it again would only stop it again. It is capped by load balancing as every start is: with no room for the charger's minimum nothing is sent, and the charge waits as a balancing pause that is resumed when there is room. Only with the car known to be plugged in, and only while the charger is off by SpotNav's own stop: a charge started or stopped at the charger since is left alone.
- **A car that takes less than the plan counts on is noticed within minutes, and the plan is made again with what it takes.** A car on a 20 A three-phase plan (13.9 kW) took about 16 A (11.2 kW): its onboard charger takes no more. The plan's watch judged the window only at its end, because load balancing had held the current a moment, and the window just passed; the power learned of the car had nine minutes from one plug-in, too few to plan with. So even a quiet night would have ended near 96 % instead of 100 %. Now, once the car has drawn clearly less (below 90 %) than the current in force gives for about six steady minutes after it ramps up, that is taken as the car's own limit: the rest is planned again at once with it, buying more quarter-hours or starting earlier, and a departure it can no longer make is told as "will not be ready" as before. Only the plan's own current is judged: while load balancing, the import target or the sun holds the current back, or when the plan's current is within 2 A of the charger's least current, nothing is, so a hold is never taken for the car's limit. Nothing is judged either once the car is at 90 % or more, where it slows down by itself.
- **What is learned of the car's power is planned with from the next plug-in.** It is kept per vehicle, and one plug-in with a quarter of an hour of steady charging, or the car's limit seen in it, is now enough; before, it took two plug-ins and twenty minutes at the full current, which a site whose load balancing often holds the current might never reach. The energy is now counted between the energy register's own posts, so a register that posts every few minutes no longer makes a short stretch look slower or faster than it was. A plan never counts on more than the charger's current gives, nor on less than the car was seen to take in this plug-in.
- **"Fill" and a target in percent work for a car whose battery size nobody states.** A BMW reports its level and its own limit but not its battery size, and none was set, so "Fill" planned the stored 22.4 kWh instead of filling from 11 % and said the size was unknown, although SpotNav had already learned from earlier charges that the car takes about 0.83 kWh from the wall per percent. Now, when no battery size comes from a setting, the car's integration or its profile, the size learned from charging stands in: the wall kWh per percent at the usual 90 % charging efficiency (about 75 kWh for that car), so the planned energy is the learned kWh per percent times the percent to fill. One charge of the car that raised its level at least 10 points with at least 5 kWh is enough for this, and a figure that would mean a battery under 10 or over 200 kWh is never used. The status says so ("The battery size is learned: about 75 kWh"), and the car's battery size setting shows the learned size beside "Not set" and offers it as a suggestion, without storing it. A size that is set or reported always wins.
- **"Fill" and a target are planned again as soon as the car's battery size, limit or level becomes known.** After a restart the BMW's sensors had no value yet, so "Fill" was planned with the stored 22.4 kWh, and it stayed so after the car reported its level and battery size: only reloading SpotNav planned the 55 kWh to the car's limit. Two things kept it: a new level planned again only for a target, never for "Fill", and the battery size and limit were read again only every five minutes and never listened to. Now a new level plans "Fill" again as it does a target, the car's battery size and limit are read again the moment its level arrives, and a change of either (one that becomes known included) plans again at once.

## Improved

- **The car is a button over the action bar, as in the app.** It shows the car's level and opens a dialog with the rest, where the age of the car's last reading is now told instead of on the card.
- **The price chart keeps its room in a card of fixed height.** The status is cut to two lines first, and the chart never gets lower than 100 px (120 px where it fits). The readout of a tapped hour is drawn over the chart's bottom edge, so its line reaches the chart.
- **Pointing at the charts is easier.** The price chart picks the hour by the pointer's position across alone and follows a hovering mouse, and dragging a finger over the history bars moves the readout from bar to bar.
