SpotNav for Home Assistant 1.21.0 lets a charger that can switch phases charge on one phase when the sun does not cover three, and no longer lets a charging decision that fails pass in silence.

## Solar charging

- **Phase switching for solar.** A three-phase charge cannot go below 6 A on each phase, about 4.1 kW, so with less sun than that a solar charge either drew the rest from the grid or did not run. On a charger that can switch phases, the new setting **Phase switching for solar** (the card's settings, under the charger) lets SpotNav charge on one phase instead, from about 1.4 to 3.7 kW, and go back to three phases when the sun covers them.
  - It switches down to one phase when the sun stays short of three phases for the solar stop delay (5 minutes) but covers one; when it does not cover one either, the charge stops as before. It switches back up when one phase is at its maximum and the sun has covered three phases with a margin for 10 minutes.
  - At most three switches per plug-in, at least 15 minutes apart, so a car is not interrupted often. The charger makes a short pause to switch; for 90 seconds after a switch that pause is not taken for the car ending the charge.
  - Only solar charging switches. A planned period, a person's Start, a top-off and the car's minimum charge level always charge on three phases, and the phases are set back before such a charge starts.
  - While the charge is on one phase, load balancing reckons the charger as a single-phase charger on the phase it draws on, and the status says so, for example *Solar · 1 phase · 2.9 kW*.
  - Off by default: some cars charge badly after a phase change. It is offered only on a charger wired for three phases on a site with solar, and for now only on **Peblar** (its *Force single phase* switch; the charger's smart charging strategy must be *Default*). More chargers will follow.

## Charging decisions

- **A decision that fails is told.** When the current logic could not decide an event for a charger, the previous logic decided it with only a debug line. Now the first such failure is a warning in the log and every one is counted in the charger's diagnostics and debug info. When it happens three times within a day on the same charger, a Repairs issue says what you can do: send the debug info with an issue, or switch the charger to the previous logic under its Configure. The issue goes away by itself after a day without a failure.
