SpotNav for Home Assistant 1.19.0 follows a single-phase charger to the phase it charges on, lets load balancing act on sites whose meter measures each phase's current directly, and stops a load-balancing pause from keeping Home Assistant busy while the charger answers.

## Load balancing

- **A single-phase charger follows its phase.** A single-phase charger whose phase was not chosen got no load balancing at all, and a fixed phase is wrong where something moves the charge between phases: an Easee Equalizer, for one, puts a single-phase car on L1, L2 or L3 from charge to charge. Now **Automatic (follows the charger)** is the default. While the charger draws, SpotNav reckons it on the phase its own measured current is on, once the site meter bears that out and it has held for 10 seconds; before a start, or without a clear reading, on the phase with the least room. A phase chosen by hand still applies. The choice is in the site's **Configure**, in the step **Wiring for …**. An Easee with Automatic is read on all three terminals so its phase can be told.
- **Active control on sites that measure current directly.** Load balancing could only act on sites whose current is derived from each phase's power, because lowering a charger is only safe on a phase that imports, and a current sensor alone does not say which way the current flows. Now a site whose meter gives each phase's current with a sign (a Tibber Pulse, for one) and has a total grid power sensor gets active control too, once SpotNav has confirmed the sign: three readings in a row where the phases add up to the meter's total and point the same way. Until then, and on meters whose sign cannot be confirmed, it calculates and shows, as before. On these sites a lowering is never larger than the room on its phase, whoever writes a charge's current (the sun or load balancing) is settled when the charge starts, and a charge that loses its confirmation is still lowered on an overload and never raised.
- **A pause no longer keeps Home Assistant busy.** When load balancing paused a charger that still reported charging while the stop reached it (an OCPP charger takes a few seconds to answer), the pause decided itself again and again, hundreds of times a second, for up to 15 seconds and longer for a charger that does not answer. The charger got no extra commands, but Home Assistant was kept busy. The pause now waits for the charger's answer.
